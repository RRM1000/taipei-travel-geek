Most visitors to Taiwan don't need a visa at all - but the rules depend entirely on your nationality, and it's worth double-checking before you book flights. This guide covers who qualifies for visa-free entry, what you need at the airport, and how to extend your stay if you decide not to go home on time.

This page is a general overview, not immigration advice. Entry requirements change and vary by nationality, so always confirm your specific situation on the Bureau of Consular Affairs website or with your nearest Taiwan representative office before you travel.

Visa-Exempt Entry

Taiwan has one of the more generous visa-free programmes in Asia. Passport holders from a large number of countries - including the US, UK, most of the EU, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, Japan and South Korea - can currently enter Taiwan visa-free for up to 90 days for tourism purposes. A smaller number of other nationalities are eligible for shorter visa-free stays (commonly 14 or 30 days).

Because the list of eligible countries (and the number of days granted) changes from time to time, don't rely on second-hand summaries, including this one, to make a final decision - check your eligibility directly on the Bureau of Consular Affairs site linked below before booking.

Passport Requirements

A few basics that catch first-time visitors out:

  • Your passport should be valid for the duration of your stay in Taiwan. Some airlines and immigration desks are stricter than the official minimum, so having at least 6 months of validity remaining is the safest bet if you can.
  • You'll usually be asked for proof of an onward or return flight when checking in or at immigration.
  • Make sure you have at least one blank passport page for the entry stamp.

Online Arrival Card (TWAC)

Since 1 October 2025, Taiwan has done away with paper arrival cards altogether. Every foreign visitor now has to submit a Taiwan Arrival Card (TWAC) electronically - there's no paper form handed out on the plane anymore, so don't wait for one.

You have two ways to do this, and the difference matters:

  • Fill it in online before you fly - takes about five minutes from your phone or laptop, any time in the 7 days before you land.
  • Fill it in at the airport if you haven't done it in advance - you'll be pointed to a QR code at the immigration hall and have to complete the whole form on your phone standing in the queue, or use one of the kiosks. It works, but it's slow, fiddly on a small screen after a long flight, and holds up the queue behind you too.

Doing it in advance is the difference between walking straight up to the immigration desk and standing in the arrivals hall filling in a form on your phone with everyone else who didn't.

How to Fill It In Online

  • Go directly to twac.immigration.gov.tw - this is the official National Immigration Agency site. The NIA has specifically warned about fraudulent copycat sites that charge a fee for what is a completely free government service, so don't just search and click the first result.
  • You can submit it any time from 7 days before your flight up until you land.
  • Have these ready before you start: your passport, flight number and arrival date, and the address of where you're staying in Taiwan (your hotel confirmation email has this).
  • The form asks for standard details - name, passport number, nationality, date of birth, occupation, purpose of visit - all matching your passport exactly.
  • Once submitted, you'll get a confirmation. You don't need to print anything - at immigration, the officer scans your passport and your TWAC submission is pulled up automatically. It's still worth keeping a screenshot as a backup in case of any system issue.

Fill it in for every traveller in your group individually, including children - each passport needs its own TWAC submission.

What Happens at Immigration

Arrival at Taoyuan Airport is generally quick and straightforward:

  • Follow signs for Foreign Passports / Non-ROC Nationals at immigration.
  • You'll have your photo and fingerprints taken as part of standard entry procedures.
  • Have your passport and onward flight details ready to show if asked - your TWAC submission is pulled up automatically when your passport is scanned.
  • Customs after baggage claim is normally a walk-through unless you're carrying something to declare.

Once you're through, see the Airport MRT guide linked below for the quickest way into central Taipei.

Extending Your Stay

This is one of the most common points of confusion: most visa-exempt stays cannot be extended. If you want to stay in Taiwan longer than the visa-free period allows, you generally need to either leave and re-enter (rules on how soon you can do this vary), or apply for an actual visa in advance of your trip rather than relying on the visa-exempt programme. Don't assume you can simply visit an immigration office locally and add extra days - policies here are stricter than in many other countries.

If You're Not Visa-Exempt

If your nationality isn't covered by the visa-exempt programme, you'll need to apply for a visitor visa at a Taiwan representative office (there's no embassy, so look for a "Taipei Economic and Cultural Office" or similar in your country) before you travel. Processing can take at least several working days, so apply well ahead of your trip.
