Jiufen (九份) is a small mountain village on Taiwan's north-east coast, about an hour from Taipei. It's famous for its steep, narrow streets strung with red lanterns, its old tea houses overlooking the coast, and for being one of the reported inspirations behind the town in Studio Ghibli's Spirited Away. It's one of the most popular day trips from Taipei, and for good reason - but it's also easy to visit at the wrong time and spend most of it stuck in a crowd. This guide covers how to get there, when to go, what to see, and how to combine it with nearby Shifen and the Northeast Coast.

How to Get There

Jiufen isn't on the MRT network, so you'll need to combine the train or metro with a bus. There are three main options:

Direct Bus (Easiest)

Take the Taipei Metro Brown Line to Zhongxiao Fuxing Station, then follow signs to Exit 1. From street level, catch bus 1062 directly to Jiufen (look for the "Jiufen Old Street" 九份老街 stop). The journey takes around 60-70 minutes depending on traffic, and you can pay with an EasyCard.

Train + Bus

Take a TRA (regular rail, not HSR) train from Taipei Main Station to Ruifang Station (瑞芳), then walk out to the bus stop just outside the station and catch bus 788, 825, 856 or 965 up to Jiufen Old Street. This route is slightly cheaper and lets you combine Jiufen with Shifen or Houtong, both of which are also reachable from Ruifang.

Taxi

A taxi from Taipei takes around 45 minutes and costs roughly NT$1,200-1,500 one-way. Worth it if you're in a group of 3-4 or want to skip the bus queue back to Taipei in the evening, which can be long.

By Guided Day Trip

If you'd rather skip the buses and transfers entirely, a guided day trip will pick you up and drop you back in central Taipei, and takes care of timing your visit around the crowds. It's also the easiest way to combine Jiufen with Shifen or the Northeast Coast in a single day without working out train and bus connections yourself. See the tour options further down the page for a few options depending on what you want to combine it with.

Best Time to Visit

Jiufen gets extremely busy, especially on weekends and public holidays, when the narrow streets can turn into a slow shuffle. A few tips to avoid the worst of it:

  • Go on a weekday if you can - weekends are dramatically busier.
  • Arrive before 10am or after 4pm. The lanterns look their best around dusk, and the crowds thin out noticeably once the tour buses head back.
  • Avoid Chinese New Year and other major holiday periods entirely unless you enjoy queuing.
  • Bring cash - most food stalls and small shops don't take cards.

Jiufen Old Street

Jiufen Old Street (基山街) is the main pedestrian lane running through the village, packed with food stalls, souvenir shops, and photo spots. It's steep and narrow in places, and can get genuinely crowded at peak times, so patience helps. Give yourself at least 90 minutes to walk it properly and duck into the side alleys.

The most photographed spot is the row of red lanterns above the stepped alley next to A-Mei Tea House - this is the view most commonly associated with the town's Spirited Away comparisons. It's best photographed around dusk when the lanterns are lit but there's still some light in the sky.

Tea Houses

Jiufen's tea houses are one of the best reasons to slow down and spend a couple of hours here rather than just passing through. Most serve Taiwanese oolong tea sets with a small snack, and many have balcony seating looking out over the rooftops to the sea.

  • A-Mei Tea House - the most famous (and busiest), right by the lantern-lined steps.
  • Old Street tea houses further up the hill tend to be quieter with similar views, worth seeking out if A-Mei has a queue.

Expect to pay around NT$300-600 per person for a full tea set, which usually comes with instructions on how to brew it properly - it's a nice way to rest your legs after the walk up.

What to Eat

A few Old Street stalls and snacks worth seeking out:

  • Taro balls (芋圓) - Jiufen's signature dessert, chewy taro and sweet potato balls served in a hot or cold sweet soup. Try them at the shop most people queue for near the start of the Old Street.
  • Fish ball soup - a simple, cheap, warming option if you're visiting on a cooler day.
  • Grilled squid on a stick - one of the classic night-market-style snacks you'll see being cooked along the street.

Yin Yang Sea & Golden Waterfall

A short bus or taxi ride from Jiufen (or a stop on most guided tours) is the Yin Yang Sea (陰陽海), a bay where mineral deposits from the old Jinguashi mines turn part of the water a distinct yellow-brown against the deep blue of the open sea. Nearby is the Golden Waterfall, stained a similar yellow colour by the same mineral runoff. Both are free to view from the roadside and worth 20-30 minutes if you have your own transport or are on a tour that includes them.

Combining with Shifen & the Northeast Coast

Jiufen pairs naturally with a few other spots along the same rail line and coast, so it's worth planning a full day if you can:

  • Shifen - famous for its waterfall and sky lantern releases, reachable via the Pingxi Branch Line from Ruifang.
  • Houtong Cat Village - a small former mining village now home to a large resident cat population, also on the Ruifang line.
  • Shifen Old Street and the Northeast Coast - if you have a car or join a tour, the coastal road past Bitou Cape offers some of the best sea views in the area.
