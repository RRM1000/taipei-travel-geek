Taipei has thousands of cafes, but far fewer that want you to stay for an afternoon with a laptop. Plenty now set a time limit, a minimum spend or a "no laptops at weekends" rule, and some of the prettiest have no WiFi at all. These are the cafes that still welcome people who come to work or study, with the rules you'll actually meet at each one.

💡 The Quick Verdict: Cafes to Work From in Taipei

  • Look for 不限時 ("no time limit") and 插座 ("plugs") on the door or menu. Even then, expect to buy at least one drink each.
  • Block & Cafe near Taipei Main Station is the most work-friendly cafe in the city: a plug at every seat, no time limit, and open until 1am.
  • Homey's cafe near Zhongxiao Fuxing is the best in the East District, and Dreamers Coffee on Nanjing West Road the best around Zhongshan.
  • Weekends are the problem. Several cafes that are relaxed on weekdays bring in a 90-minute to 3-hour limit on Saturdays and Sundays.
  • Louisa is the cheap fallback that's on almost every corner, but rules vary by branch and WiFi isn't guaranteed.

At a Glance

Rules and hours as reported in October 2026. Cafes change them often, so check the notice by the counter when you arrive.

Block & Cafe (町咖啡), Taipei Main Station

If you only remember one cafe on this list, make it this one. Block & Cafe is a second-floor room on Taiyuan Road, a few minutes' walk north of Taipei Main Station, and it's set up for people who've come to get something done: there's a plug at every seat, free WiFi, no time limit and even a paid printer. It's quiet enough to read in, and it opens from 8am until 1am, which makes it one of the few places you can still work late at night.

It sells drinks only, so eat first. The entrance is easy to miss: the ground floor is an office, and the small sign sits under the arcade.

Address: 2F, 50 Taiyuan Road (太原路50號2樓)  ·  Open in Google Maps

Homey's cafe, Zhongxiao Fuxing

Homey's is upstairs in an old apartment block off Dunhua South Road, about four minutes from Zhongxiao Fuxing station. It's a converted flat full of books and daylight, with a plug at every seat, free WiFi and no time limit as long as everyone orders a drink. It's the best East District option for a long session, and a much calmer one than the cafes on the main shopping streets of the East District.

Address: 2F, No. 36, Lane 236, Section 1, Dunhua South Road (敦化南路一段236巷36號2樓)  ·  Open in Google Maps

Youmoutoohana Coffee (羊毛與花)

Its name is almost impossible to pronounce, but Youmoutoohana has been one of Taipei's best laptop cafes for years and still sets no time limit. The original branch is just off Yongkang Street, five minutes from Dongmen exit 5, with plenty of plugs, good WiFi and quiet music. It takes phone bookings on weekdays only.

For a full day of work, the newer Huashan branch (新生北路一段62巷17號), about ten minutes from Zhongxiao Xinsheng and close to Huashan 1914 Creative Park, is the better choice. It has no time limit on weekdays or weekends, single seats with dividers, and plugs. It takes no bookings at weekends, and one listing shows it closed on Thursdays. There's more about the Yongkang branch in my full review of Youmoutoohana.

Dreamers Coffee Roasters, Zhongshan

Dreamers is the one cafe on this list that both English-language and Taiwanese reviewers recommend for working. Its Nanxi branch on Nanjing West Road is three to five minutes from Zhongshan station, bright and spread over two floors, with a plug at every seat upstairs, fast WiFi and no time limit for anyone who orders a drink or something to eat. It opens at 8am, and prices sit somewhere between Louisa and Starbucks. Dreamers has other branches, including one near Zhongxiao Fuxing, if you're elsewhere in the city.

Address: 86 Nanjing West Road (南京西路86號)  ·  Open in Google Maps

Le Promenoir (4F 劇場咖啡), Ximending

The fourth-floor cafe inside Zhongshan Hall, a listed 1930s building on the edge of Ximending, is the most atmospheric place to work in this part of town. There's no time limit, WiFi and plugs at most seats, and views across the old city towards the North Gate. It closes at 7pm and fills up even on weekdays, so arrive early. Expect to pay around NT$180–190 for a coffee.

Address: 4F, 98 Yanping South Road (延平南路98號4樓), inside Zhongshan Hall  ·  Open in Google Maps

Arthere Café (上樓看看), Xinyi

Arthere is the pick near Taipei City Hall and the Xinyi malls, about five minutes from City Hall exit 4. It sets no time limit on any day and has plugs by almost every table, but it's popular for brunch as well, so it's often full and can be chatty. Book ahead by phone (02-8787-1782); without a booking, you may not get a seat.

Address: No. 6, Alley 3, Lane 165, Section 5, Zhongxiao East Road (忠孝東路五段165巷3弄6號)  ·  Open in Google Maps

Yaboo Café (鴉埠咖啡)

If you like working in near silence, Yaboo, a few minutes from Dongmen exit 5, is still one of the quietest cafes in Taipei, and almost everyone there has come to work. There are plugs by most seats, free WiFi and good food. It used to have no time limit, but it now asks people to leave after three hours when it's full. It's cash only and takes no bookings, so arrive before 2pm at weekends.

If it's full, its sister cafe Yaboo II on nearby Lishui Street (麗水街9巷10-1號, open from 2pm, closed Thursdays) is the overflow. There's more in my full review of Yaboo.

Louisa

Taiwan's biggest coffee chain has hundreds of branches, so there's almost always one nearby, and the bigger ones have plenty of seats and plugs. The company sets no time limit, but some branches put up their own notices (re-order if you leave your seat for more than 20–30 minutes, no outside food), and there's no reliable Louisa WiFi network, so bring your own data. It's the cheapest sit-down option on this list. See my Louisa guide for the nicest branches, and our guide to cheap coffee in Taipei for current prices.

Fine for a Coffee, Less So for Work

Two cafes that used to be on this list have tightened their rules:

  • Café Costumice near Zhongxiao Dunhua is still one of the coolest-looking cafes in the city, with a courtyard garden. But it now has a two-to-three-hour limit at weekends, a minimum spend and a 10% service charge, and only a few seats near plugs. It's better for an evening drink than a working day.
  • Cho Café (町‧如固咖啡) in Ximending makes excellent coffee, but it now has a minimum spend of NT$150 on weekdays and NT$250 at weekends, a 90-minute limit at weekends, and closes on Wednesdays and Thursdays.

Common Questions

What does 不限時 mean?

"No time limit". It's the phrase to look for on Taiwanese cafe menus, Instagram pages and review sites. It almost always comes with a minimum order of one drink per person. Watch for 限時 (time limit), 低消 (minimum spend) and 插座 (plugs).

Do Taipei cafes have free WiFi?

Most do, but not all: some of the city's most fashionable specialty cafes have none. Taipei's free city WiFi (TPE-Free) covers many public places, but it's slow. A local SIM or eSIM is the reliable answer.

Where can I work late at night?

Block & Cafe near Taipei Main Station is open until 1am, and Yaboo and Café Costumice until midnight. Most others close by 10pm, and some (like Le Promenoir) by 7pm.

Is it OK to study in Starbucks or Louisa?

Yes. Both chains are used by students all day, and neither sets a company-wide time limit. Big branches are your best bet for plugs, but expect noise, and check for a branch notice about leaving your seat.
