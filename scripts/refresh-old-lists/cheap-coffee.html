Coffee in Taipei has got more expensive. Louisa, the city's go-to cheap chain, put up its prices in January 2026, and Starbucks now charges NT$125 for a medium latte. But a decent cup still costs less than NT$50 if you know where to look, and the cheapest of all is in the convenience store on almost every corner.

💡 The Quick Verdict: Cheap Coffee in Taipei

  • Cheapest: 7-Eleven and FamilyMart, both NT$35 for a medium Americano and NT$45 for a medium latte, hot or iced.
  • Cheapest with a seat: Louisa, now NT$60 for an Americano and NT$80 for a latte (medium).
  • Best coffee for the money: Ray Cafe near Zhongxiao Dunhua, hand-roasted, with most drinks around NT$100 or less.
  • Bring your own cup for NT$5 off at 7-Eleven and FamilyMart.
  • Starbucks is about three times the price of a convenience-store coffee.

What Coffee Costs in Taipei

Hot drinks, list prices before any offers, October 2026. Large sizes are usually NT$10–20 more.

Convenience Stores: 7-Eleven & FamilyMart

The cheapest decent coffee in Taipei comes out of the machine at the back of a convenience store. 7-Eleven's City Café and FamilyMart's Let's Café charge exactly the same: NT$35 for a medium Americano and NT$45 for a medium latte (NT$45 and NT$55 for a large), and iced costs the same as hot. City Café uses its own blend, roasted in Taiwan. (You may read that it uses the same beans as Starbucks. It doesn't: both brands belong to the same Taiwanese group, which is where the rumour started, and Starbucks Taiwan publicly denied it in 2017.)

Some 7-Eleven stores also sell a pricier specialty range, CITY PRIMA, from around NT$80 for a medium Americano. Hi-Life and OK Mart charge much the same as the big two for a large coffee.

Deals: all four chains run frequent offers: buy one get one free, a second cup at a discount, or bundles of prepaid cups (寄杯) that can work out at under NT$25 a cup. The best bundles are sold through each chain's app, which usually means signing up with a Taiwanese phone number. Without one, look for the offer poster by the till, as in-store deals are applied at the counter. All four convenience-store chains take the EasyCard.

Louisa

With hundreds of branches across Taipei, Louisa is the city's everyday coffee chain, and still the cheapest place to sit down with a proper espresso drink. It raised prices on about 90 items in January 2026, and a medium Americano is now NT$60 and a medium latte NT$80 (about NT$75 and NT$100 for a large). The coffee is consistent from branch to branch, there's a good breakfast menu of toasties and bagels, and the bigger branches have plenty of seats and plugs, which is why they also appear in our list of cafes to work from.

Louisa's app sells prepaid cups at a discount, and some mornings bring small early-bird discounts. See my Louisa guide for the nicest branches to visit.

Ray Cafe

For better coffee than a chain without paying specialty-cafe prices, Ray Cafe is a small, independent roaster a short walk from Zhongxiao Dunhua exit 3, beside the Ming Yao department store. It roasts its own beans, sets no time limit, and most drinks still come in around NT$100 or less. Its Belgian waffles are worth adding. It's open from 08:30 to 17:30 on weekdays and 10:00 to 18:00 at weekends, and there's no toilet. There's more in my full review of Ray Cafe.

Yolo's Cafe

Yolo's is a small local chain of cheap and cheerful cafes with seats, plugs and WiFi, and a menu of simple snacks to go with the coffee. It now has four branches: the original on Tong'an Street near Xinyi Anhe, one off Zhongxiao East Road near Zhongxiao Dunhua, one in the Hung Kuo Building on Dunhua North Road, and one in Zhishan. There's more in my Yolo's Cafe review.

Common Questions

How much is a coffee in Taipei?

NT$35–45 at a convenience store, NT$60–100 at the cheaper chains, NT$100–140 at Starbucks, and NT$120–250 at a specialty cafe. Our guide to the best coffee shops in Taipei covers the specialty end.

Is convenience-store coffee in Taiwan any good?

Better than you'd expect. It's made fresh from beans in an espresso machine, not from instant, and Taiwanese office workers drink it every day. A latte from 7-Eleven or FamilyMart is a perfectly decent drink for NT$45.

How do you order coffee in Taiwan?

Menus at the chains are usually bilingual. 美式 is an Americano, 拿鐵 a latte, 熱 hot and 冰 iced, and 中杯 / 大杯 medium and large. Iced drinks come with a lot of ice unless you ask for 少冰 (less ice).
