With dozens of Indian restaurants, Taipei isn't short of choice, but plenty are forgettable. These are the ones Indian residents, food writers and the Michelin Guide keep coming back to, from a Bib Gourmand in Tianmu to a fully vegan curry house, with the halal, vegetarian and South Indian options picked out.

💡 The Quick Verdict: Indian Food in Taipei

  • Best overall: Saffron in Tianmu, the only Indian restaurant in the 2026 Michelin Guide (Bib Gourmand). Worth the trip.
  • Best in the centre: Oye Punjabi on Yanji Street, small, Indian-run and home-style.
  • Halal: TAJ near Zhongxiao Fuxing, halal-certified and a Bib Gourmand from 2020 to 2024. Mayur Indian Kitchen is halal too.
  • Vegan: 3 Idiots Toast & Curry, with no eggs, dairy or onion and garlic.
  • Most Taipei Indian restaurants are North Indian. For dosas, go to Mayur or Nataraj.
  • Book at weekends, and expect NT$400–600 a head with naan and a drink.

Saffron Fine Indian Cuisine (番紅花印度美饌)

Saffron is the only Indian restaurant in Taipei in the 2026 MICHELIN Guide, as a Bib Gourmand: good cooking at a fair price. It has held that every year since the guide first came to Taipei in 2018. The cooking is North Indian, and Michelin singles out the murgh Nawabi (chicken curry) and gosht Lahori (mutton curry), with the meat charred in the tandoor first. The saffron curry and the phirni (rice pudding) are the dishes local reviewers mention most, and there are plenty of vegetarian dishes.

It's in Tianmu, a fair way from any MRT station, so take a bus or taxi from Zhishan. It's open for lunch and dinner but, on most reports, dinner only on Mondays. Booking is a good idea (it takes reservations through OpenTable), and there's a 10% service charge. There's more in my full review of Saffron.

Address: 38-6 Tianmu East Road, Shilin (天母東路38之6號)

Oye Punjabi (哦耶！旁遮普)

Oye Punjabi serves homely Punjabi cooking in the heart of the East District, cooked by an Indian team. Its naan is probably the best I've had in Taipei: thick, chewy and buttery. The dal tadka is sensational, and reviewers also rate the malai (white-sauce) tandoori chicken. It's popular and the original room is small, so phone ahead. It now has a second shop nearby, at 18, Lane 137, Yanji Street.

It's on Yanji Street, between Sun Yat-sen Memorial Hall and Zhongxiao Dunhua stations, open for lunch and dinner and closed on Mondays (the second shop opens daily). There's more in my full review of Oye Punjabi.

Address: 121 Yanji Street, Da'an (延吉街121號)

Balle Balle (巴雷巴雷印度餐廳)

Balle Balle has been one of Taipei's best-loved Indian restaurants for well over a decade, and it's still the place Indian residents and expat food writers name most often. It's owner-run, with an Indian kitchen, and serves Punjabi classics: butter chicken (about NT$380), lamb rara, tandoori chicken and a smoky baingan bharta (roast aubergine), which reviewers single out. Budget NT$500–600 a head. Book, especially at weekends.

Address: 12 Guangfu North Road, Songshan (光復北路12號), near Bade Road  ·  Open in Google Maps

TAJ (泰姬印度餐廳): Best for Halal

TAJ is one of the few halal-certified restaurants in Taipei, and it was a Michelin Bib Gourmand every year from 2020 to 2024. It's North Indian, and its tandoori chicken is the dish every review mentions, along with the dal makhani, mutton biryani and palak paneer. A deluxe thali is about NT$390 and a curry about NT$350, plus a 10% service charge. It's a few minutes from Zhongxiao Fuxing station, open for lunch and dinner.

Address: 1, Lane 48, Section 4, Civic Boulevard, Da'an (市民大道四段48巷1號)  ·  Open in Google Maps

Mayur Indian Kitchen (馬友友印度廚房)

Mayur is the biggest Indian restaurant group in Taiwan, with more than a dozen branches, so there's likely to be one near you. It's halal, and unlike most places in Taipei it cooks South Indian dishes as well as North: the masala dosa with sambar is a good reason to come. The original MIK-1 on Keelung Road by Ren'ai Road in Xinyi is the flagship, and the MIK-3 branch on Jinan Road, near Zhongxiao Xinsheng, is entirely vegetarian and vegan. Chicken tikka masala, palak paneer and a long list of naans are the regulars' picks.

Flagship: 350-5 Section 1, Keelung Road, Xinyi (基隆路一段350之5號)  ·  Vegetarian: 64-2 Section 2, Jinan Road (濟南路二段64-2號)  ·  All branches in Google Maps

Out of India (印渡風情): Best Value

Tucked into the lanes near Shida (National Taiwan Normal University), Out of India is the cheap and cheerful pick, and one that Indian residents recommend. It's North Indian, its pani puri (crisp shells you fill and eat in one) is a favourite, and the set menu for two is good value.

Address: 26, Lane 13, Pucheng Street, Da'an (浦城街13巷26號)  ·  Open in Google Maps

3 Idiots Toast & Curry (三個傻瓜印度蔬食): Best Vegan

From the same owner as Out of India, 3 Idiots is a fully vegan Indian restaurant: no meat, eggs or dairy, and none of the five pungent vegetables (onion, garlic and their relatives) that Buddhist vegetarians avoid. That makes it a rare place where every diner in a mixed group can order anything. The samosas, lassi and "cheese" naan get the best reviews. Its Nanjing branch is on Changchun Road, near Nanjing Fuxing station. For more meat-free options, see our vegetarian restaurants guide.

Nataraj Indian Cuisine (納達吉印度料理)

Nataraj moved in 2024 from Nanjing East Road to a bigger room on Keelung Road in Xinyi, about six minutes from Taipei City Hall station exit 1. Its menu mixes North and South Indian dishes, and reviewers rate the red tandoori chicken, lamb shank curry and sea bass curry. It's open for lunch and dinner, closed on Mondays, and takes cash or JKOPay only.

Address: 127 Section 1, Keelung Road, Xinyi (基隆路一段127號)  ·  Open in Google Maps

Where to Find South Indian Food

Almost every Indian restaurant in Taipei cooks North Indian food. For dosas, idli and sambar, Mayur and Nataraj are the reliable choices. The one true South Indian kitchen is Amma's Kitchen (清奈媽媽印度廚房), which serves Chennai home cooking at 2, Lane 357, Section 2, Heping East Road, near Technology Building station, but it opens only on certain days, so check before you go.

Common Questions

Is there a Michelin-recommended Indian restaurant in Taipei?

One: Saffron Fine Indian Cuisine in Tianmu, a Bib Gourmand in the 2026 MICHELIN Guide Taiwan. TAJ was a Bib Gourmand from 2020 to 2024 but dropped out of the 2025 guide.

Where can I find halal Indian food in Taipei?

TAJ near Zhongxiao Fuxing is halal-certified, and Mayur Indian Kitchen's branches are halal too.

Is Indian food in Taipei spicy?

Usually milder than in India, because kitchens tone it down for local tastes. Most places will cook it hotter if you ask; say so when you order.

How much does an Indian meal cost in Taipei?

Expect NT$300–450 for a curry, NT$70–120 for a naan, and NT$400–600 a head for a full meal. Some restaurants add a 10% service charge.
