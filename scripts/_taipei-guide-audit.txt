
<p>The capital city of Taiwan, Taipei is a city that's often overlooked by travellers touring the Asian region, but it's one that definitely deserves a visit.</p>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="/media/2023/01/Taipei-1024x678.jpg" alt="" class="wp-image-9783"/></figure>



<p>A haven for food, with many <a href="/category/markets">Night Markets</a> packed with delicious food stalls, it also has unmissable attractions such as the iconic <a href="/taipei-101">Taipei 101</a>, the staggering <a href="/chiang-kai-shek-memorial-hall">Chiang Kai-shek Memorial Hall</a>, and the <a href="/national-palace-museum">National Palace Museum</a>, which contains the largest collection of ancient Chinese artefacts in the world.</p>



<p>The city of Taipei, where the main bulk of the attractions are, is just a few kilometres across, so you don't need to travel far to experience most things. The fast and reliable MRT system also extends further to New Taipei City, so you can easily escape the confounds of the city centre to experience more rural parts.</p>

They're expected to give handouts of NT$5,000 ($165) to 500,000 tourists during 2023/24. Details are still sketchy currently but I will update as soon as I hear more.</p></blockquote>

<h2 id="People">What are Taiwanese People Like?</h2>



<p>Taipei is the safest capital city I have ever visited. In the four years I've lived here, I've never encountered any trouble. The people here are generally warm, friendly and helpful. </p>



<p>Even though Taiwan's population are mainly of Chinese ethnicity, they actually bear a closer resemblance to their neighbour Japan. Much like the Japanese, many Taiwanese people are reserved and courteous, and will go out of their way to help you.</p>



<hr class="wp-block-separator has-alpha-channel-opacity"/>



<div style="height:42px" aria-hidden="true" class="wp-block-spacer"></div>



<h2 id="English">Is English Spoken in Taiwan?</h2>



<p>Chinese is the most widely spoken language in Taiwan, although the further south you go, they'll tend to speak more Taiwanese.</p>



<p>English is taught in most schools, and there are many cram schools dedicated to teaching English. The younger population have a better grasp of English than the older generation.</p>



<p>Local restaurants and shops will often not speak much English but larger shops and restaurant chains will usually be able to understand English.</p>



<p><strong>Almost all signs are translated to English</strong> however, and their underground system (MRT) is all translated, as are the announcements on the MRT and buses.</p>



<p>If you do not speak Mandarin Chinese, it may be helpful to bring a phrasebook or download a translation app to help you communicate during your visit to Taiwan. You could also try some <a href="/the-best-guided-tours-in-taipei">guided tours</a> to some of Taipei's top attractions and night markets.</p>



<hr class="wp-block-separator has-alpha-channel-opacity"/>



<div style="height:42px" aria-hidden="true" class="wp-block-spacer"></div>



<h2 id="Crowded">How Crowded is Taipei?</h2>



<p>Taipei has a population of around <strong>2.7 million</strong> people.</p>



<p>It's a busy city, especially during commuting times and on weekends but it doesn't get nearly as crowded as places such as Hong Kong.</p>



<p>Taipei Main Station gets especially busy during peak times, so expect to queue for trains here, and expect to be crammed into carriages.</p>



<p>There are many <a href="/best-parks-in-taipei">parks</a> and other more peaceful areas you can use to escape from the busy streets.</p>



<hr class="wp-block-separator has-alpha-channel-opacity"/>



<div style="height:42px" aria-hidden="true" class="wp-block-spacer"></div>



<h2 id="Weather">Weather in Taipei</h2>



<h4>December to March</h4>



<p>The coldest time of year, this period is also the driest. When the sun is out, the temperature is actually very pleasant (mid 20s Celsius) but when it rains the temperature can drop to single figures. This is the best time to visit if you want to avoid rain but don't mind being chilly some days.</p>



<h4 id="mce_36">April to May / October to November</h4>



<p>Spring and Autumn periods can vary from very hot to quite chilly. One day you'll find it quite humid, while other days you'll need a jumper or coat. If you can't bear the intense heat of the summer, these months are probably your best bet as they can be very nice. You can also expect extended periods of rain.</p>



<h4>June to September</h4>



<p>Being in a <strong>subtropical climate</strong>, Taipei can get very hot and humid in the summer, with temperatures in the low 40s not out of the question. There can also be spells of rain lasting days.</p>



<p>There's also a risk of <strong>typhoons</strong>, with generally 5 or 6 each year. The eye of the storm will pass in a few hours, but during this time you'll be stranded in your hotel, and most places will close down for a day or sometimes two days.</p>

<h2 id="Pollution">Air Pollution in Taipei</h2>

<p>Your best option is to purchase a pre-paid SIM card directly from either airport. There are deals in the city but they're generally not as good and harder to find.</p>

<h2 id="Cash">Cash and Credit Cards</h2>



<p>You'll need to purchase some local currency, the <strong>New Taiwan Dollar </strong>(NT$100 is approximately USD$3.23).</p>



<p>If you're unable to obtain some prior to your trip, you can get some at either airport, although like most airports, you'll end up paying slightly more. Alternatively, you can exchange money from one of the many <strong>Bank of Taiwan</strong> branches (note that banks in Taipei generally <strong>close at 15:30</strong>) for a more favourable rate.</p>



<p><strong>Credit cards</strong> are accepted at malls and larger restaurants, but local places (and night markets) will require cash.</p>



<p>There are many <strong>cash machines</strong> around the city and in most convenience stores.</p>

<h2 id="Budget">Budget</h2>



<p>The cost of living in Taipei isn't too expensive for a capital city (much cheaper than Hong Kong and Singapore), and you'll be expected to spend between NT$3,000 - NT$5,000 per person, including mid-range accommodation, per day.</p>



<p>Of course this is largely dependent on how much you are prepared to splash out.</p>



<p>Eating at night markets, local restaurants or within the food courts found in shopping malls will generally be much cheaper than eating out in mid-range restaurants. You can easily feed 2 people at local places for under NT$250 (US$9), whereas a decent restaurant meal will likely set you back at least 3 or 4 times more.</p>



<p>Public transportation is very cheap, with the MRT and buses costing around NT$20 - NT$30 per trip.</p>

<p>If you can afford more time, and maybe incorporate a <a href="/best-day-trips-from-taipei">day trip or two</a>, then 1 week would be enough but there's plenty to see here for a much longer stay.</p>

<h2 id="Transport">Transport Options</h2>



<p>I would strongly recommend getting an <strong>EasyCard </strong>before you travel anywhere in Taipei. It can be used on all types of public transportation, including the Airport Express, and offers a 20% discount on all MRT travel.</p>



<blockquote class="wp-block-quote"><p><a href="/taiwan-easycard">Click here to read more about the EasyCard</a></p></blockquote>



<p>An excellent alternative to the EasyCard is the <strong>Taipei Fun Pass</strong>. This comes in four variations, some of which will give you free use of all public transportation in Taipei, along with free entry to many top attractions!</p>



<blockquote class="wp-block-quote"><p><a href="/taipei-fun-pass">Click here to read more about the Taipei Fun Pass</a></p></blockquote>



<h3 id="Transport-Airports">Airports</h3>



<p>Two airports serve Taipei:</p>



<ul><li>Taoyuan Airport</li><li>Songshan Airport</li></ul>



<p>The main airport, <strong>Taoyuan</strong>, is located a few miles west of the city centre, but both terminals are connected via the <strong>Airport Express</strong> to Taipei Main Station.</p>



<p>The <a href="/taoyuan-airport-mrt">Airport Express</a> is the quickest way to travel between Taoyuan Airport and Taipei Main Station, with trains running<strong>&nbsp;</strong>every 15 minutes, taking approximately&nbsp;36 minutes to travel&nbsp;in either direction.</p>

<p>The second smaller airport, <strong>Songshan </strong>is much closer to the city centre. It is connected to the underground MRT system and takes only 10 minutes to reach the city (20 minutes to Taipei Main Station).</p>

<h3 id="Transport-City">City</h3>



<p>Taipei has a few options for getting around, including:</p>



<ul><li><a href="/mrt">MRT (underground system)</a></li><li>City Buses</li><li>Taxis</li><li><a href="/taipei-youbike">YouBikes</a></li><li><a href="/taipei-sightseeing-bus">Sightseeing Buses</a></li></ul>



<p>The best way to get around Taipei is to use the <a href="/mrt"><strong>MRT system</strong></a>, which is efficient and convenient, with trains running frequently and serving a wide area, although this can get busy during peaks periods.</p>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="/media/2019/10/MRT-Rush-Hour-1024x750.jpg" alt="MRT Rush Hour" class="wp-image-5487"/><figcaption>MRT</figcaption></figure>



<p><strong>City Buses </strong>are a good second choice, and you may need to use these to visit attractions such as the National Palace Museum which is not located near an MRT station.</p>



<p>There are many <strong>Taxis </strong>found in Taipei, and Uber can now also be used. Taxi fares are NT$70 for the first 1.25km, with every additional 200m charged at NT$5. The fares will rise during late nights/early mornings.</p>



<p>There are hundreds of <a href="/taipei-youbike">YouBike</a> stations located around the city if you’re feeling more energetic. This is a bike rental scheme set up by the government to help reduce traffic congestion and encourage people to get fit. They're easy to use, although you'll require a Taiwanese phone number and an EasyCard to rent one.</p>



<p>There are also many <a href="/taipei-sightseeing-bus">Sightseeing Buses</a> that will take you to many of Taipei's top attractions are bustling areas. This has two routes, one which circles around central Taipei, with the other route traveling to the Shilin where the <a href="/national-palace-museum">National Palace Museum</a> is situated, along with the <a href="/shilin-night-market">largest night market in Taiwan</a>. </p>



<p>Please visit my <a href="/taipei-public-transport">Public Transportation section</a> for a comprehensive guide.</p>



<h3 id="Transport-Outside">Outside Taipei</h3>



<p>If you desire to travel outside of Taipei, maybe to see another city or to see the beautiful scenic areas in Taiwan, you have a few options.</p>



<p>Firstly, the MRT system does extend to <a href="/xinbeitou">Xinbeitou</a> and <a href="/best-day-trips-from-taipei">Danshui</a>. Both of these beautiful districts are a few miles north of Taipei and are worth visiting -Xinbeitou for it's hot springs and Danshui for it's beautiful riverside walk and sunset.</p>



<p>If you want to travel to another city down the west coast, the quickest and most convenient way to do so is by the <a href="/taiwan-high-speed-rail-hsr-discounts-klook">Taiwan High Speed Rail</a> (THSR).</p>



<p>The THSR travels to every major city on the west side of Taiwan all the way down to Kaohsiung&nbsp;in under 100 minutes. There are also some great offers for tourists holding non-Taiwanese passports. Please visit my <a href="/taiwan-high-speed-rail-hsr-discounts-klook">full guide for further details</a>.</p>



<p>Taipei also has a number of Tourist Shuttle Buses which travel from various locations in Taipei. These visit beautiful areas such as Yangmingshan National Park, Juifen and other scenic townships and areas of natural beauty including waterfalls and hot springs. </p>



<p>See my <a href="/taipei-fun-pass">Taipei Fun Pass</a> post for more details.</p>

<h2 id="Stay">Where to Stay</h2>



<p>There are several areas in Taipei that are popular with tourists and expats, each with its own distinct character and amenities. </p>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="/media/2019/05/Ximen-4-1024x768.jpg" alt="Ximen" class="wp-image-713"/><figcaption> Ximending</figcaption></figure>



<p>Some of the best areas to stay in Taipei include:</p>



<ul><li><strong>Xinyi District</strong>: This is the modern business and financial hub of Taipei, home to the iconic Taipei 101 skyscraper. The area is known for its shopping, dining, and nightlife, as well as its convenient location near many of the city's major landmarks. Many of the best hotels in Taipei are found within this district.</li><li><strong>Zhongzheng District</strong>: This is the historical and cultural centre of Taipei, home to many of the city's temples and museums. The area is more laid back and residential than Xinyi, but still has plenty of restaurants and shops, while there is some nice cheap accommodation here, including many hostels found close to Taipei Main Station. It's the best place to stay for connectivity to other areas.</li><li><strong>Daan District</strong>: This is a trendy and upscale area known for its fashionable boutiques and cafes. It is also home to several universities, so the area has a youthful and energetic vibe. There are some excellent boutique hotels in this district, and great connection to other areas in Taipei.</li><li><strong>Songshan District</strong>: This is a more traditional and residential area, known for its relaxed atmosphere and local markets. It is home to the <a href="/raohe-night-market-foody-heaven">Raohe Street Night Market</a>, one of the oldest and most popular night markets in Taipei. Transportations options are more limited in this district however.</li><li><strong>Wanhua District</strong>: Home to the <a href="/ximending">Ximending shopping are</a>a, which is known for its street food, shopping, and nightlife. Wanhua District is a lively and vibrant part of Taipei with a rich history and culture. Ximending is also a very popular destination for the younger generation and the LGBT community.</li><li><strong>Beitou District</strong>: If you would prefer to stay in a more rural setting, I would recommend staying in Beitou for a night, as the region has many hotels with natural hot springs to relax in.</li></ul>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-full"><img src="/media/2023/01/taipei-district-map.jpg" alt="" class="wp-image-9784"/><figcaption>Taipei Districts</figcaption></figure>



<p>For a deep-dive into the best areas and hotels to stay in during your trip, <a href="/best-areas-and-hotels-to-stay">I have a post to help you out</a>.</p>



<p>Ultimately, the best area to stay in Taipei will depend on your personal preferences and the purpose of your trip. If you want to be in the heart of the action, Xinyi, Wanhua or Daan might be the best choice. If you prefer a more laid-back atmosphere, Songshan or Beitou might be better options.</p>

<h2 id="Visit">Which Places to Visit in Taipei</h2>



<h3 id="Attractions">Attractions</h3>



<p>Taipei is a city with many attractions, both cultural and modern. Here are a few places you might want to visit during your trip:</p>



<ul><li><a href="/national-palace-museum">National Palace Museum</a>: This museum is home to a vast collection of Chinese art and artefacts, including ceramics, jade, and bronze. It is one of the most visited museums in Taiwan and a must-see for anyone interested in Chinese history and culture.</li><li><a href="/taipei-101">Taipei 101</a>: This beautiful skyscraper was once the tallest building in the world and remains an iconic symbol of the city. You can go to the observation deck for panoramic views of Taipei, or visit the mall at the base of the building for shopping and dining.</li><li><a href="/chiang-kai-shek-memorial-hall">Chiang Kai-shek Memorial Hall</a>: This is a national monument and popular tourist destination in Taipei, dedicated to the former President of the Republic of China. The hall features a large bronze statue of Chiang Kai-shek, as well as an exhibition on the history of the Republic of China.</li><li><a href="/shilin-night-market">Shilin Night Market</a>: This is the largest and most popular night market in Taipei, known for its food stalls and shopping. You can find all kinds of street food and snacks here, as well as clothes, electronics, and other goods.</li><li>Temples: Taipei has dozens of temples located all over the city. <a href="/longshan-temple">Longshan Temple</a> is one of the oldest and most beautiful, while <a href="/hsing-tian-kong-temple">Hsing Tian Kong Temple</a> is one of the most sacred temples in Taiwan.</li><li>Creative Parks: There are 2 really cool creative parks in Taipei, with gift shops, cafes, independent cinemas and usually a few exhibitions showcasing art or design. Both <a href="/huashan-1914-creative-park">Huashan 1914 Creative Park</a> and the <a href="/songshan-cultural-and-creative-park">Songshan Cultural and Creative Park</a> are worthy of a visit.</li><li>Taipei Zoo &amp; Maokong Gondola: <a href="/taipei-zoo">Taipei Zoo</a> is one of the best zoos in Asia, while the <a href="/maokong-gondola">Maokong Gondola</a> is a fantastic cable car ride into the mountains. One of the stations for the gondola is inside the zoo.</li><li>Hiking: Taipei is surrounded by mountains to north and east, including Elephant Mountain and Tiger Mountain, both of which offer fantastic views of the city including Taipei 101</li></ul>

Taipei 101 is also found here.</li><li><a href="/yongkang-street">Yongkang Street</a>: Recently voted the 4th coolest street in the world, this is a great place to try some bubble tea or shaved ice dishes, and perfect for gift shopping. <h2 id="Eat">Where to Eat &amp; Drink in Taipei</h2>



<p>Night Markets are your best bet to experience some traditional food and snacks.</p>



<p>While <a href="/shilin-night-market">Shilin Night Market</a> is the largest and has the most variety of food stalls, <a href="/raohe-night-market-foody-heaven">Raohe Night Market</a> is arguably the best for food. If you prefer to visit a less touristic night market, then <a href="/michelin-food-stands-at-night-markets#Linjiang">Linjiang Street Night Market</a> has a lot to offer in terms of food.</p>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="/media/2019/10/Raohe-Night-Market-12-1024x730.jpg" alt="Raohe Night Market" class="wp-image-5430"/><figcaption>Raohe Night Market</figcaption></figure>



<p>If you want to experience some Michelin recognised food at night markets, I have a post which will help you find the <a href="/michelin-food-stands-at-night-markets">locations of all of these at every night market</a>.</p>



<p>To experience slightly more refined Taiwanese food, then the very popular <a href="/din-tai-fung">Din Tai Fung</a> should definitely be on your list. This world-renowned chain of restaurants originated in Taipei and is best known for its xiaolongbao (soup dumplings). You can find branches all over the city, although finding a table is sometimes difficult since Din Tai Fung do not have a table reservation service.</p>



<blockquote class="wp-block-quote"><p><a href="/din-tai-fung">Read my post to find out the best way to skip the queues here</a></p></blockquote>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="/media/2019/10/Din-Tai-Fung-7-1024x712.jpg" alt="Din Tai Fung" class="wp-image-5759"/></figure>



<p>For a traditional Taiwanese breakfast, I would recommend <a href="/fuhang-soy-milk">Fu Hang Soy Milk</a>. This local breakfast spot is known for its fresh soy milk and savoury fried dough sticks. The restaurant is always busy, but it's worth the wait for a taste of these Taiwanese favourites. If you can't face queuing an hour for breakfast, then <a href="/yong-he-soy-milk-king">Yong He Soy Milk King</a> is a great alternative.</p>



<p>If you prefer a slightly fancier brunch, there are some <a href="/best-brunch-in-taipei">nice brunch spots</a> found around the city, including the very delicious (but very popular) <a href="/the-antipodean-specialty-coffee">Antipodean Specialty Coffee</a>.</p>



<p>I would also recommend trying some beef noodle and braised pork rice dishes, both of which are very popular in Taiwan. <a href="/yong-kang-beef-noodles">Yong Kong Beef Noodles</a> and <a href="/jin-feng-braised-pork-rice">Jin Feng Braised Pork Rice</a> are the most famous and best in the city.</p>



<p>If you're a lover of seafood, then you should check out <a href="/addiction-aquatic-development">Addiction Aquatic Development</a>. Taipei's largest fish market also doubles as a fantastic dining experience, with many seafood restaurants and a large grill area where you can pick your own seafood, meat and vegetables.</p>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="/media/2019/10/Addiction-Aquatic-Development-14-1024x723.jpg" alt="Addiction Aquatic Development" class="wp-image-5552"/><figcaption>Addiction Aquatic Development</figcaption></figure>



<p>Vegetarians are well catered for in Taipei. Aside from the night markets, there are some <a href="/best-vegetarian-restaurants-in-taipei">excellent vegetarian and vegan restaurants</a>. </p>



<p>I sometimes find that I need some respite from all the fried foods on offer, but luckily there are some nice <a href="/healthy-restaurants-that-taste-great">healthier options</a>.</p>



<p>Please visit the <a href="/category/eat">Eat</a> pages for a full list of my favourite places to eat in Taipei.</p>



<h3>Drink</h3>



<p>There are hundreds of tea shops selling a large variety or hot and chilled teas, including bubble tea, the city's most famous drink, which is made with sweetened tea, milk, and chewy tapioca pearls.</p>



<p>Taipei is home to a thriving <a href="/tag/coffee">cafe culture</a>, offering a wide range of options for coffee lovers and casual drinkers alike. From traditional tea houses to modern, hipster-style cafes, there's something for everyone in Taipei's cafe scene. </p>



<p>Visitors can enjoy a traditional Taiwanese tea ceremony in a cosy tea house, complete with beautiful teapots, intricate tea sets, and an array of teas to choose from. For coffee aficionados, Taipei has plenty of options, from independent cafes to international chains, all serving up high-quality coffee and espresso drinks. If you're on a tight budget, there are some <a href="/cheap-coffee">nice cheap cafes</a> selling espresso-based coffees for under NT$50.</p>



<p>If you're looking for something a bit stronger, Taipei's night markets offer an array of traditional Taiwanese liquor, such as rice wine and herbal liqueurs, made from ingredients like ginseng and goji berries.</p>



<p>There are also some great bars in Taipei, including <a href="/best-bars-in-taipei">expat bars with some nice happy hour deals</a>, or you could try some of Taipei's <a href="/best-places-to-drink-craft-beer-taipei">homegrown craft ales</a>, or cocktails bars such as the popular <a href="/woo-taipei">Woo Taipei</a> and the speakeasy <a href="/hanko-60">Hanko 60</a>.</p>

<h2 id="Tips">General Tips for Taipei</h2>



<p>There are three items you should probably never be without.</p>



<ul><li>Umbrella</li><li>Mosquito Spray</li><li>Easycard</li></ul>



<p>Even when it isn't raining, the Sun's rays can often be too much to bear and you can easily be caught out by a sudden rain shower out of nowhere.</p>



<p>Taipei isn't short on Mosquitoes, even during the winter months, so Mosquito Spray is essential!</p>



<p>An <a href="/taiwan-easycard">Easycard</a> is incredibly useful to get around, and can even be used to purchase goods from many shops.</p>



<p>If you are going to spend the day shopping, I would also recommend you take your <strong>passport</strong> with you, as you'll need this if you want to take advantage of the VAT refund scheme.</p>



<p>During the summer months, you'll also need <strong>sunscreen</strong> (and sunglasses)!</p>
