Taipei rewards a slower trip more than most Asian capitals - the food alone could fill a week. This itinerary is built around three full, walkable days that cover everything you shouldn't skip, plus two extension days if you have more time. Each day is grouped by district so you're not criss-crossing the city, and every stop links through to a full guide with opening hours, prices and tips.

How Many Days Do You Need?

Three days is enough to cover Taipei's essentials without rushing - Days 1 to 3 below give you the temples, night markets, Taipei 101, and the food scene, all within the city itself.

Five days lets you slow down and add a proper museum day plus either a hot spring afternoon in Beitou or a day trip out to Jiufen - Days 4 and 5 cover both options so you can pick whichever suits you.

If you only have one day, see my separate 24 Hours in Taipei guide instead, which is built for a single-day layover or stopover.

Before You Go

A few things worth sorting before Day 1, all of which make the rest of this itinerary run smoother:

  • Check whether you need a visa and how the online arrival card works - see my Taiwan Visa & Entry Requirements guide.
  • Pick up an EasyCard at the airport or any MRT station - you'll use it for the metro, buses, YouBikes and even some convenience store purchases. Full details in my EasyCard guide.
  • Get a local SIM or eSIM so you have maps and translation on hand from day one - see my Taiwan SIM card comparison.
  • Get from the airport into the city on the Taoyuan Airport MRT - it's the fastest and cheapest option.
  • Decide where to base yourself - this guide to the best areas and hotels breaks down each neighbourhood's pros and cons.

Itinerary at a Glance

Day 1 - Da'an & Central Taipei

A gentle first day that eases you into the city - a famous monument, Taipei's best-loved street for food, and a big park to walk it all off.

Morning: Chiang Kai-Shek Memorial Hall

Start at the Chiang Kai-Shek Memorial Hall, one of Taipei's most recognisable landmarks. If you time it right, you can catch the hourly changing of the guard ceremony. The surrounding plaza and gardens are free to walk around, and the National Theatre and Concert Hall either side make for good photos.

Lunch & Afternoon: Yongkang Street

Yongkang Street is a short walk or MRT ride away, and is one of the best food streets in Taipei. It's also home to one of the original Din Tai Fung branches, if you want to tick off the world-famous xiaolongbao here rather than at Taipei 101. If you'd rather queue less, Yong Kang Beef Noodles nearby is a local favourite and far less busy.

Spend the rest of the afternoon browsing the surrounding lanes - tea shops, dessert stores and small boutiques fill the streets around here.

Evening: Daan Forest Park

Finish the day with a walk through Daan Forest Park, Taipei's answer to Central Park. It's a nice, quiet way to end a first day before an early night to shake off jet lag.

This day closely follows my Da'an walking route, which also adds the weekend-only Jianguo Flower Market if you're doing this on a Saturday or Sunday. If you'd rather go deeper into the history and architecture around the Memorial Hall instead of heading to Yongkang Street, my Zhongzheng walking route covers that side of the district, including the National Taiwan Museum.

Day 2 - Old Taipei: Wanhua, Ximending & Datong

This is Taipei's oldest, most atmospheric district - temples, historic shophouses, and one of the best night markets in the city, all connected by a short MRT ride or a pleasant walk along the river.

Morning: Longshan Temple & Bopiliao

Start early at Longshan Temple, one of Taipei's oldest and most important temples - arrive before the crowds if you can, and don't miss the intricate roof carvings. From there it's a short walk to the Bopiliao Historical Block, a preserved stretch of Qing-dynasty and Japanese-era shophouses that gives a real sense of what old Taipei looked like.

Lunch & Afternoon: Ximending

Ximending is Taipei's answer to Harajuku - pedestrianised streets packed with street food, fashion, and youth culture. Grab lunch from one of the food stalls, then browse the shops. Don't miss The Red House, a beautifully restored Japanese-era market building now home to craft shops and cafes.

Late Afternoon: Dadaocheng

Head north into the Datong district for a change of pace - Dadaocheng Old Street is lined with restored merchant houses, traditional tea and herbal medicine shops, and a genuinely different atmosphere to Ximending. See my Datong walking route for a full route through the area.

Evening: Ningxia Night Market

Finish the day at a night market for dinner. Ximending and Datong are both close to smaller markets, but if you want the full experience, see my guide to Michelin-recognised food stands at Taipei's night markets to plan exactly what to eat.

Day 3 - Xinyi & Songshan

Taipei's most modern face - skyscrapers, luxury malls, and a creative park built inside an old tobacco factory, finishing at one of the city's best night markets.

Morning: Taipei 101

Head up Taipei 101 in the morning before the queues build - book tickets online in advance to skip the line entirely. If you want to add the new outdoor Skyline 460 platform, that needs a separate reservation, so book it before you arrive.

Lunch & Afternoon: Xinyi Shopping District

The Xinyi Shopping District surrounding Taipei 101 has more than a dozen malls, department stores and a huge range of restaurants for lunch. It's also one of the best areas in the city for an afternoon of shopping if that's your thing.

Late Afternoon: Songshan Cultural & Creative Park

A short MRT ride away, the Songshan Cultural and Creative Park is a former tobacco factory turned into a relaxed space of design shops, exhibitions and a pond garden - a nice contrast to the malls you just left.

Evening: Raohe Night Market

Finish the day at Raohe Night Market, one of Taipei's oldest and most famous, right by Songshan. Enter through the temple gate at the east end and work your way down - my guide covers exactly which stalls have been recognised by the Michelin Guide.

This day is essentially my Songshan/Xinyi walking route laid out with timings - that route starts at Sun Yat-Sen Memorial Hall instead, if you'd like to see the changing of the guard there before continuing on to Songshan and Xinyi.

Day 4 - Shilin & Museums

If you have a fourth day, this is where to spend it - Taipei's best museum, a former presidential residence, and one of the biggest night markets in Taiwan, all in the same district.

Morning: National Palace Museum

The National Palace Museum holds one of the largest collections of Chinese artefacts in the world - give it at least two to three hours, and consider an audio guide to make sense of the sheer scale of the collection.

Afternoon: Chiang Kai-Shek Shilin Residence

A short taxi or bus ride away, Chiang Kai-Shek Shilin Residence was the former president's home, with well-kept gardens that are pleasant to wander even if you're not deep into the history.

Evening: Shilin Night Market

Finish at Shilin Night Market, the largest in Taiwan and many visitors' first stop for Taiwanese street food. It's touristy, but for good reason - see my guide for which stalls are actually worth the queue.

Day 5 - Beitou & Tamsui, or a Day Trip

Your fifth day is a good one to leave the city centre. There are two very different options depending on what you're after - pick one, or split the day between the first two if you're short on time.

Option A: Beitou Hot Springs & Tamsui Sunset

Take the MRT out to Xinbeitou for a morning of hot springs - visit the Beitou Hot Spring Museum and Thermal Valley before or after a soak. In the afternoon, continue on the red line out to Tamsui for the old street, riverside walk, and one of the best sunsets near Taipei.

Option B: Jiufen Day Trip

If you'd rather see the lantern-lit streets that inspired part of Spirited Away, spend the day on my full Jiufen day trip guide, which also covers combining it with Shifen's waterfall and sky lanterns. This one works well either self-guided by bus, or as a guided tour that picks you up and drops you back in the city.

Short on Time? Maokong Instead

If a full day out of the city doesn't appeal, the Maokong Gondola is a half-day option - a scenic cable car ride up into the hills south of the city, ending at a cluster of tea houses with views back over Taipei.

Have a Sixth Day? Gongguan

None of the days above cover Gongguan, home to National Taiwan University's campus, so if you're staying longer it's worth its own day. My Gongguan walking route takes in the campus, the Water Park, Treasure Hill Art Village, and finishes at Gongguan Night Market - it's a long one at 8-10 hours, so start early.

General Tips

  • Book Taipei 101 and the National Palace Museum online in advance - both get busy, and pre-booked tickets skip the queue.
  • Night markets are best on weeknights if you want to avoid the biggest crowds - Friday and Saturday nights get packed.
  • Wear comfortable shoes. Every day above involves a lot of walking, even with the MRT doing the heavy lifting between districts.
  • Leave one evening unplanned. Taipei's spontaneous finds - a random alley bar, a late-night dessert shop - are often the best part of a trip.
  • If you're combining several paid attractions, check whether the Taipei Fun Pass works out cheaper than buying individually.
